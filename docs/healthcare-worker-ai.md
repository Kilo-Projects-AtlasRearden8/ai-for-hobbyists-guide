# AI for Healthcare Workers (Education Only)

This page shows how healthcare workers — like nurses, aides, and medical assistants — can use free AI for non-clinical tasks. AI is never a substitute for clinical judgment, and it must never give or replace medical advice.

**Remember:** Never paste patient names, dates of birth, medical record numbers, diagnoses, photos, or any identifying health information into an AI tool. See `docs/privacy.md`. HIPAA and workplace privacy rules still apply.

## Appropriate uses (non-clinical, education only)

- Explaining common medical terms in plain language for your own learning
- Drafting non-clinical emails, schedules, or shift notes (with no patient details)
- Learning how to search reliable health information (for example, government health sites)
- Organizing continuing-education notes and certificates
- Practicing how to explain a procedure to a patient using made-up examples

## What AI must NOT do in healthcare

- Diagnose or treat any patient
- Suggest medications, doses, or care plans
- Interpret lab results or imaging
- Replace a clinician's assessment
- Handle real patient data

## Safe practice example

Do not say:

> "My patient John Doe, born 1/1/1970, has [condition]. What should I do?"

Instead, for learning only:

> "Explain what [medical term] means in plain language, using a made-up example patient. Do not give medical advice."

## Workplace rules

- Follow your employer's policy on AI use.
- Never enter patient data into a public AI tool.
- When in doubt, ask your supervisor or compliance officer.

## Reliable health information

Look for sources ending in `.gov` (for example, CDC.gov, MedlinePlus.gov) or major medical institutions. AI can point you toward them, but verify yourself.
