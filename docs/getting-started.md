# Getting Started with Free AI

You do not need to pay for AI, and you do not need a powerful computer. This guide shows you how to start using free AI models, step by step, in plain English.

## Step 1: Pick a way to use AI

There are three beginner-friendly options. Pick one and try it.

| Option | What it is | Best for | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website chatbot | A free AI you talk to in your web browser | Quick questions, trying things out | Free |
| Free phone app | An AI assistant app on your phone | Using AI on the go | Free |
| Local AI | A free model that runs on your own computer | Maximum privacy, works offline | Free |

### Option A: Website chatbot (easiest)

1. Open your web browser.
2. Go to a free AI chat site. Common free options include Google Gemini, Claude by Anthropic, or Microsoft Copilot. (Links change over time — search for the official site if one does not load.)
3. Create a free account if asked. Use an email address you already have.
4. Type your question in the chat box and press Enter.

### Option B: Phone app

1. Open your phone's app store.
2. Search for the official app of a free AI assistant (for example, Claude, Gemini, or Microsoft Copilot).
3. Install it, open it, and sign in with a free account.
4. Start a new chat and type your question.

### Option C: Local AI (most private)

1. Download a free local AI runner such as Ollama or LM Studio from its official website.
2. Install it like any other program.
3. Download a small free model (the app will suggest one, such as Llama 3.2 or Gemma).
4. Chat with the model inside the app. Nothing leaves your computer.

## Step 2: Ask your first question

Start small. AI works best when you give it a clear task and a little context.

**Try this:** "I am a beginner knitter. Suggest three easy first projects and what supplies I need for each."

Then try a follow-up: "Pick one of those and write the steps out like a recipe."

## Step 3: Write better prompts

You do not need special syntax. Talk to AI like you would to a helpful friend who does not know your life. A good prompt has four parts — use as many as fit:

1. **Role:** Who should the AI be? ("Act like a patient craft teacher.")
2. **Task:** What exactly do you want? ("List five scrapbook page ideas for a wedding album.")
3. **Context:** What do they need to know? ("I have never used a Cricut. I like nature themes.")
4. **Format:** How should the answer look? ("Use a bulleted list. Keep each step under two sentences.")

**Before:** "Help with crafts."

**After:** "Act like a patient craft teacher. I want to make a simple birthday card for a child. List five ideas, the supplies needed for each, and the steps in order. I have never made cards before, so keep it beginner-friendly."

## Step 4: Save what works

AI conversations can disappear. When an AI gives you something useful:

- Copy it into a notes app or a document.
- Or print it out (see `project-planner.md`).
- Label it with the date and the project name.

## Step 5: Stay safe

Read `docs/privacy.md`. The short version: never paste personal info into an AI tool. No names, addresses, passwords, bank details, or health information.

## Where to find free models

All of the following have a free tier or are free to use:

- **Google Gemini** — gemini.google.com (web and app)
- **Claude by Anthropic** — claude.ai (web and app)
- **Microsoft Copilot** — copilot.microsoft.com (web and app)
- **Ollama** — ollama.com (runs models locally on your computer)
- **LM Studio** — lmstudio.ai (runs models locally on your computer)
- **Hugging Face** — huggingface.co (browse and try free models in your browser)

Use the official websites or apps. Avoid lookalike sites that ask for payment or promise "unlimited" AI for a fee.

## What AI is good at for hobbies

- Brainstorming project ideas
- Explaining steps in plain language
- Making shopping lists from a project plan
- Turning messy notes into a tidy list
- Explaining game rules or craft techniques
- Writing emails to sellers or organizers

## What AI is not good at

- Knowing current local prices or store stock
- Remembering things from last week unless you tell it again
- Giving medical, legal, or financial advice
- Replacing hands-on practice

## Your first project

Pick one tiny thing this week:

- Ask AI for three easy scrapbook layouts.
- Ask AI to explain a board game rule you keep forgetting.
- Ask AI for a checklist before starting a small home repair.

Then move on to the hobby guides: `crafting.md`, `collecting.md`, `gaming.md`, or `diy.md`.
