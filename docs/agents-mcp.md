# AI Agents and MCP for Beginners

You may hear the words "agent" and "MCP" in AI conversations. This page explains them in plain English, with no jargon required.

**Remember:** Never paste personal info into an AI tool or agent. See `docs/privacy.md`.

## What is an AI agent?

Think of an AI assistant as someone who answers questions. Think of an AI agent as someone who can also do things for you — like searching a website, filling out a form, or moving files — with your permission.

A simple example:
- Assistant: "Here is how to list an item on eBay."
- Agent: "I found your item details, wrote the listing, and posted it for you (after you said OK)."

Agents are still new. As a beginner, treat them like a helpful intern: useful, but always check their work before they do anything important.

## What is MCP?

MCP stands for Model Context Protocol. It is a free, open standard that lets an AI assistant connect to tools and data sources you control.

Analogy: MCP is like a USB port for AI. Just as USB lets your computer plug into many different devices, MCP lets an AI plug into many different tools — your notes, your calendar, your spreadsheet — without each one needing a custom connection.

## Why beginners should care

- You can connect free, local AI to your own hobby data (collection lists, project planners, budgets) without giving a company your data.
- MCP servers are often free and open-source.
- You stay in control: the AI only touches what you let it.

## A safe first experiment

Do not connect an agent to your bank, email, or medical accounts. Start with read-only, local data.

1. Set up local AI (see `docs/local-ai.md`).
2. Try a free, open-source MCP "server" that reads files on your computer only.
3. Ask the AI to summarize your own project-planner notes — nothing leaves your machine.

## What agents are not good at yet

- Making purchases or payments for you
- Accessing accounts you did not explicitly connect
- Giving medical, legal, or financial advice
- Working perfectly without mistakes
