# Chess Tools with AI

This page shows how to use free AI to enjoy and learn chess. It is written for beginners and casual players.

**Remember:** Never paste personal info into an AI tool. See `docs/privacy.md`.

## How AI can help with chess

- Explaining the rules and how each piece moves
- Suggesting beginner openings and why people play them
- Explaining common tactics (forks, pins, skewers) with simple examples
- Translating chess notation into plain language
- Suggesting puzzles to practice

## Example prompts

> "I am new to chess. Explain how the knight moves, with a simple example."
> "What is the difference between a pin and a fork in chess? Use an example I can picture."
> "Suggest three beginner-friendly chess puzzles to practice, and describe them so I can set them up on a real board."

## What AI cannot do

- Play a strong game against you (use a dedicated chess app or website for that)
- Analyze a real game deeply unless you describe the moves clearly
- Replace practice on a board or app

## Free chess resources

- Lichess.org (free, no ads, play and puzzles)
- Chess.com (free tier, play and lessons)
- Your local library may have chess books and clubs

## Playing over the board

AI can explain ideas, but chess is best learned by playing. Try a local club, a friend, or a free app.
